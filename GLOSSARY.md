# Glossary

The names this project uses for its own things, in code, docs and reviews. Where the code name differs, it's in parentheses.

## The flow

- Core flow (Aidan, Oct 28): record, then the preview, then the editor (auto zoom and cursor follow, the drawn cursor, background and size, speed and blur), then export. Launch is this path. Anything else ships only once it works, has a clear purpose and is in the app, or else it shows as Coming soon.
- Record bar (`src/pages/RecordBar.tsx`, `windows/bar.rs`): the pill at the bottom center of the screen, just above the Dock. In setup it has Hide (×, which hides the bar while the app keeps running in the menu bar), the mode, camera and microphone, and the Settings menu (Settings… and Quit). The camera button is grayed out with a Coming soon tooltip, and the app sends no camera to capture (Aidan, Oct 28). While recording it shows the timer, Restart, Pause and Stop.
- Menu-bar icon (`tray.rs`, `icons/tray.png`): the app's mark (rings with a pointer) in the macOS menu bar. It stays there from launch until Quit. Its menu has New recording (shows the bar and opens the picker in the last mode, or brings back the preview or editor if a recording is open), Record display, Record window and Record area (the same, in that mode), Stop recording (only while recording), Settings… and Quit. New recording shows the app's mark and the three Record items show the record bar's mode icons (`icons/menu/`), drawn in the menu's text color. Tauri's menu items only take macOS's built-in image names, so `replace_native_icons` registers our pictures under four of those names (Computer, Icon view, List view, Column view) before the menu is built. Clicking the Dock icon shows the bar the same way, without opening the picker.
- Settings window (`src/pages/Settings.tsx`): the editor's Settings content (`SettingsBody`) in a small window of its own, opened from the menu-bar icon or the bar's Settings menu. Without a recording it has no "Set from this recording". Every window follows `settings-changed`, so the editor and the Settings window never undo each other's changes.
- Permissions window: the window where a new user allows Screen Recording (required) and the microphone (optional). It opens by itself until setup is finished (`hasFinishedSetup`) and whenever Screen Recording is off. Each permission's access is allowed, needs restart, not asked or off. Needs restart means Screen Recording was turned on but macOS only applies it to a new process, so Restart OpenTape (`relaunch`) quits and reopens in one step.
- Permission and restart layers: what the record bar shows while Screen Recording is off ("Review permissions" opens the permissions window) or waiting on a restart. A refused microphone doesn't stop a recording: it records without it and the bar says Microphone access is off (`microphoneRefused` in the recording status).
- Update: a newer OpenTape found by the updater. Its status lives in Rust (`UpdateStatus`: up to date, checking, available, downloading, ready, failed), so the record bar's update layer, the editor's update chip and Settings, About always agree. Later hides the bar's update layer until the bar opens again.
- Mode: what you're about to record: a display, a window or an area.
- Picker (`src/pages/Picker.tsx`, `picker/`): what opens after you choose a mode. It puts one overlay on each display and lets you point at the target.
- Overlay: the see-through window the picker places over one display. It draws the highlight or the Area box. Overlays are made once, then hidden and shown, never closed.
- Picker session (`Session`): what's being picked: the mode, the sources, the pinned window and the display holding the Area box. Rust sends it whole to every overlay whenever it changes, and works out each window's display and each display's scale once, so the overlays never derive them.
- Sources: the displays and on-screen windows the picker can pick from.
- Highlight: the orange outline on the display or window under the pointer, with everything else dimmed.
- Pinned window: a window chosen with Tab. It wins over the pointer until the pointer moves.
- Area box (`AreaBox`): the box you drag out in Area mode, with eight handles.
- Capture bar (`CaptureBar`): the pill on the highlighted target, used in all three modes. It shows the capture tile, what will be recorded, its size in pixels, the Area's ratio chip, and Record.
- Capture tile (`CaptureTile`): the 40px square at the left of the capture bar: the window's app icon, or an icon for the mode.
- Ratio chip (`RatioChip`): the Area's control on the capture bar. Its menu locks the box to 16:9, 4:3, 1:1 or 9:16.
- Safe top (`safeTop`): the distance in points from a display's top that clears the menu bar and the notch. The capture bar and the ratio menu keep below it.
- Target: exactly what will be recorded: a display, a window, or an area of a display.
- Record (a target): the picker's last step. It closes the overlays and starts recording the target.
- Countdown: with Count down before recording on in Settings (the default), Record first turns the record bar into its countdown state for three seconds. That state sits between setup and recording and shows a ring with 3, 2, 1, "Starting in 3", the target and whether the mic is on, plus Cancel. The phase stays starting. Cancel or Esc (`CountdownKey`) goes back to idle. The bar is one of the app's own windows, so the countdown never ends up in the recording.
- Record shortcut (`recordShortcut` in Settings, ⌥⇧R by default): a system-wide key that opens the picker in the last mode (like New recording) when idle, and stops while recording or paused. Settings shows it and changes it; a new shortcut needs ⌘, ⌃ or ⌥, so it can't steal typing. It's stored as text Rust parses (`Alt+Shift+KeyR`), and the menu-bar icon's New recording shows it.
- Frame: while an area or a window records, the overlay on that display stays up as the frame. It's click-through, has no controls, and dims everything but what's recorded. Not to be confused with `geometry::Frame` (a rectangle in points, and the generated `Frame` type), a video frame, or the editor's framed recording (`frameLayout`, and appearance sizes measured on a 1920-wide frame).
- Recording status (`RecordingStatus`): the one copy of the recording's state, owned by Rust. It holds the phase, the time recorded and the last error. The record bar shows it and keeps no copy.
- Phase: where the recording is: idle, starting, recording, paused or stopping. `next_phase` in `recording.rs` decides which action is allowed when. Every way a recording ends (Stop, ScreenCaptureKit stopping by itself, quitting) goes through one `stop` that finishes the file and keeps it.
- Pause: frames captured while paused are left out, so the file plays straight through.
- Pause timeline (`capture/timeline.rs`): a recording's time with its pauses cut out, on the clock ScreenCaptureKit stamps frames with. The bar's timer and the video agree because both use it. Not the editor's timeline.
- Restart: after a native alert, throws away what's been recorded and records the same target again. The recording pauses while the alert asks, so the alert never ends up in it.
- Recording preview (`src/pages/RecordingPreview.tsx`, `windows/preview.rs`): the card in the bottom-left corner after you stop. It loops the clip, and hovering shows the clip dock. The record bar hides while it shows.
- Clip dock (`ClipDock`): the morph pill at the bottom of the preview. It holds Save, Copy and Edit, then morphs to Saving, Copied, Saved with Show in Finder, or an error.
- Current recording (`current_recording.rs`): the recording you've just made, named for when it started ("2026-10-16 07.41.28"). It waits in app storage while the preview and then the editor show it. It keeps, saves, copies, edits and closes itself, and each step decides which window shows: the bar, then the preview, then the editor. Save exports an `.mp4` to the save folder (Downloads unless Settings says otherwise). Save or Export is the only way to keep it.
- Close (the preview or editor): brings the bar back and deletes the recording and its project file. Unless it was saved or exported to a file, it asks first: the preview offers Save and close or Discard, the editor Export and close (the page runs the export, then closes) or Discard.
- Editor (`src/pages/Editor.tsx`, `windows/editor.rs`): where Edit in the preview takes the recording. It has the stage, the timeline with its modifier lanes, the side panels (Background, Cursor, Sound) and Export. `bun run dev:editor` opens the test clip in `~/Downloads/OpenTape test clip/` with its cursor log, so its edits and clicks are real. Each launch starts from the default settings, like a new recording. `EDIT_VIDEO=latest` opens the newest video in Downloads and `EDIT_VIDEO=/path/to/video.mp4` any video, each copied into one folder per video.
- Stage: the editor's big view of the recording, framed the way it will export.
- Appearance (`Appearance`): how the editor frames the recording: background, padding, corners, inset, shadow and border. Sizes are measured on a 1920-wide frame, so an appearance matches at any size. The Background panel edits it, and the stage and export both draw it.
- Fill (`Fill`): the background behind the recording: a gradient, a color, or an image. The Image tab's drop zone takes a PNG, JPEG, HEIC or WebP picture, dropped anywhere on the editor or picked with browse. `add_background` copies it beside `project.json` as `background.png`, `background 2.png` and so on, so the project stays self-contained and undo can go back to an earlier picture. The fill stores only the file name (`fill.image`). On the stage and in the export (`cutout`) the picture covers the frame from its center, like CSS `cover`. With no picture yet, the Image tab shows the gradient.
- Timeline: the editor's strip under the stage: the toolbar (play and pause with Space, the time), then the ruler, the Recording track and the lanes, with the playhead across them. Labels on the ruler sit at least 96px apart.
- Cut: a split in the Recording track, shown as a 6px gap between two clips. While the cut tool is on, a scissors disc marks each one.
- Cursor (`lib/editor/cursor.ts`, `export/cursor.rs`, `macos/cursor.rs`): the cursor drawn over the recording from its cursor log (`events.jsonl`), since capture leaves the real cursor out. Every 100ms, capture also logs which macOS cursor was showing by matching the system cursor's picture. It keeps to the simple ones (arrow, hand, text beam), and anything else counts as the arrow (Aidan, Oct 25). It also logs when a key was pressed (`{"type":"key"}`, from the macOS key-press count), never which key, with no permission needed. The stage and export draw the real macOS cursor pictures (`cursor_images`), scaled so 100% is 3.375 times real size (`CURSOR_SCALE`; Aidan, Oct 25: the old 75%). Styles (`CURSOR_STYLES`, picked from tiles in the Cursor panel) are macOS (the real pictures), Pointy, Rounded, No stem (an arrowhead) and Dot. Each drawn style has its own arrow, hand and text beam (`SHAPES` in `cursor.ts`, vector paths with a hotspot each), plain black with a white edge and no shadow, written at 8× their size; Dot is a circle for every shape. `cursorPath` works out where the cursor is at every moment: a smoothing spring that leads by its own lag so the cursor reaches a button as it lights up, fading when still, and stopping at the kept video's end. Styled always tilts while moving, dips while the button is held, removes shakes and skips cursor shapes that only flash by; these have no settings (Aidan, Oct 28: trim the panel). The cursor mode (`appearance.cursor.mode`, lines in `CURSOR_MODES`) is Hidden, Native or Styled. Native (`nativeOr`) turns all of that off and shows the real cursor 1:1; Hidden draws nothing. The settings live in `appearance.cursor`, and the Cursor panel draws them from `CURSOR_SECTIONS` through `SettingControl`. The panel has Mode, then Style (the style tiles and Size), Movement (Smoothing, Hide when still, Hide outside the recording) and Clicks (the effect on left clicks and its color). Outside Styled the sections dim to 40% and take no input, and `help` shows as a tooltip on the label. The cursor has its own layer above the recording, so it can overhang the frame; with Hide outside the recording on, it fades as its tip leaves the visible recording. It sits outside the zoom's `transform: scale()`: `Cursor` applies the zoom to its own position and size, so the browser draws it at its final size instead of stretching a layer, which made it blurry.
- Kept time: the timeline's time, with deleted stretches of the recording left out. Modifiers are stored in recording time and shown in kept time, so they stay on their footage. Deleting or trimming footage shrinks the modifiers on it and removes any with under 0.5s left.
- Time map (`timeMap` in `clips.ts`): converts between recording time and kept time, built from the clips and speed regions. The timeline, playback (`useTimeMap` plays at the speed region's rate and skips cuts) and the editor's edits all read it. Output time (speed applied) comes from `outputAt` and `sourceAt` in `regions.ts`, which the zoom, cursor, export and Stage code call directly. The Stage and export both put click rings in output time, in 60 steps per half second.
- Modifier (`Region` in code): a stretch of time that changes the video, like a zoom or a speed change. Each type is one entry in `REGIONS` (label, key, hue, default length, settings), and its key adds one at the playhead. Only types whose status is ready (Zoom, Speed, Blur) can be added, from the Add menu, ⌘K or their keys. The planned types (Captions and Audio) show in the Add menu as grayed, unclickable Coming soon rows, and are left out of ⌘K and the keys. Blur (B) blurs a box on the recording (`box`, shares of the recording, centered 30% × 20% by default; `blurOf`) at Soft, Medium or Hard strength (a radius measured on a 1920-wide frame), which you drag and resize on the stage while it's open; the stage uses CSS `backdrop-filter` inside the zoom layer, and the export gets the boxes in output time (`blurs`) and blurs them with Core Image before the cut-out, so both follow the zoom. Click ring (Oct 25) became Clicks in the Cursor panel. Annotation, Highlight and Title card were dropped (Aidan, Oct 28).
- Suggestion (`isSuggested` on a region, `SUGGESTIONS` in `regions.ts`): a Zoom or Speed block the app adds itself, once per recording (`suggest`, flagged by `suggestionsMade` in the project file). Auto zoom comes from bursts of left clicks under 3s apart (`suggestedZooms`): it aims at the first click and pans to each later one (`path`, read by `aims` in `zoom.ts`, so the stage and export both pan). Typing comes from key-press marks in the cursor log, in stretches of at least 3s (`typingStretches`). Each stretch gets a 2× Speed suggestion (`suggestedSpeeds`) and also counts as a zoom moment. The key log has only times, so the video locates the typing: `typing_spots` (`export/typing.rs`) samples each stretch twice a second at 320px wide and finds the busiest changed area (16px cells, skipping frames where over 15% changed), so other changes on screen don't pull it off. `steadySpots` drops lone flicker spots far from the two spots on each side. The zoom aims at the first spot and pans as the text moves; with no spots it aims at the last click or the mouse. For every zoom, a pan is kept only if the aimed view moves at least 8% (`MIN_PAN_SHARE`), so edge repeats and tiny moves don't jitter. Between pans, a zoom whose Focus is Follow cursor (every suggested zoom; a manual one starts on Fixed point) follows the moving cursor (`zoomPath`). It groups the moving cursor's positions into a box and aims at the box's middle. The view holds still while the cursor stays inside the box, and glides only when the cursor outgrows it (wider than half the zoomed view or taller than 70%); then a new box starts. Glides use a spring (stiffness 200, damping 40, mass 2.25) behind a 200ms ease so they start gently, and read the cursor 300ms ahead so a fast cursor stays in frame. Click and typing pans start 750ms early so they arrive with the moment, and each starts a new box. The cursor counts as moving only once it travels 2% in 300ms, so a resting hand never shakes the view. While typing (from 1.5s before to 1.5s after a typing stretch) the view ignores the cursor and stays on the text. Following runs in recording time through speed changes, and the export's zoom track comes from the same function. None of these numbers were tuned to a particular clip. A suggestion works like any block. It's drawn as a dashed ghost with a ✓ (keep, `settleSuggestion`) and an ✕ (remove), and shows its question on hover. As it narrows (`suggestionContent`) it drops the name, then the buttons; at icon size the ✓ and ✕ open in a small pill below it on hover or select. Normal blocks hide their grips at icon size but stay draggable.
- Lane: one row per modifier type in use, in registry order, under the Recording track. A block only moves in time inside its lane. Lanes can be soloed (other types dim, and the stage shows only that type) or collapsed to thin bars, one at a time or all at once from the Add menu.
- No same-type overlap (Aidan, Oct 24): two blocks of one type never overlap. A drag stops at a neighbor and jumps past it once nearer the far side, a stretch stops at it, and adding inside one lands in the nearest free gap. Different types overlap freely.
- Project file (`project.json`): the edits, saved next to the video after every change, so a crash doesn't lose them. It's deleted when the recording closes. An older file gets defaults for whatever it lacks.
- Frame layout (`frameLayout` in `lib/editor/appearance.ts`): where the recording, corners, shadow and border sit on the canvas. The frame takes the Background panel's Size (Auto matches the recording; 16:9, 9:16, 1:1 and 4:5 letterbox it inside the padding). A Size other than Auto also offers Letterbox or Fill (`fit`; older projects load as Letterbox). Fill crops the recording to the padded frame's shape so a wide recording fills a phone-shaped frame (`frameCrop`, per side: how many times the letterboxed recording the frame is). Fill sets a floor on the zoom: the scale never drops below the one that fills the frame, and a Zoom block zooms further by its amount. Outside zoom blocks, the view follows the cursor with the same box and spring as a Follow cursor zoom, aiming at the typing spots while typing. The view always stays on the recording, so the background never shows inside the frame. While you drag a zoom's focus, the stage shows the whole recording and the focus box marks the area Fill will show. The stage combines the layout with the eased zoom (`zoomPath`) at the playhead, and export gets the same numbers through `exportLayout` and the zoom track, so the two match.
- Island: one of the editor's rounded areas, such as the stage and the timeline.
- Sound (`SoundSettings` in `lib/editor/sound.ts`, `project.sound`): the recording's Mute switch. Capture writes system audio as a stereo track and the microphone as a mono one, so `sound_tracks` (`export/mod.rs`) tells them apart by channel count, and the Sound panel lists only the tracks the recording has. A volume slider per track is shown grayed out and does nothing yet (Aidan, Oct 28: coming soon). A recording with no sound grays out the Sound tab and the timeline's speaker button, and their tooltip says the recording has no sound. Export mixes every track at full volume, like Save, and a muted export has no sound track (`muted`). The speaker button in the timeline toolbar is separate and only mutes playback.
- Rail: the column of icons at the left of the side island. Its top tabs switch the panel (Background, Cursor, Sound), then a grayed-out Camera tab marked Coming soon; its bottom tabs are app-wide (shortcuts, GitHub, which opens the repository through `open_repo`, and Settings).
- Panel: what the rail shows beside it: one effect's settings, in sections of field rows.
- Export settings (`ExportSettings`): format, frame rate, resolution, quality and where the export goes. The Export button's chevron opens them; the button itself exports with them.

## Shared pieces

- Coming soon: a control that's shown but grayed out and does nothing, with a Coming soon tooltip or note, so people can see what's planned (Aidan, Oct 28). It's the alternative to shipping something half-working. Today: the record bar's camera, the rail's Camera tab, the Background panel's Wallpapers tab, the Sound panel's volume sliders, and the planned modifiers (Captions and Audio) in the Add menu.
- Audio (modifier, planned): a stretch of time with its own sound settings, such as a lower volume or muted sound for one part of the clip. It adds no music.
- Captions (modifier, planned): words on screen for what's said in the recording.
- Pill (`Pill`): the rounded bar shape every bar in the app uses.
- Morph pill (`MorphPill`): a pill that glides between states of different widths, like the record bar's setup and recording states, or the clip dock. Each state keeps its own GPU layer so its text never shakes. A morph label (`MorphLabel`) does the same for one piece of text inside it, like the bar's device names: its width glides and its text cross-fades, and it tells the morph pill its final width (`data-morph-width`) so the pill's edge and the label start together and stay in step.
- Glass: the native frosted view behind the record bar and the capture bar, owned by Rust in `macos/glass.rs`. A page can only blur its own content, so the blur of the apps behind is native.
- Panel: a non-activating macOS panel (`NSPanel`). The bar, overlays and preview are panels, so they float over every space, including full-screen apps, without taking focus. Closing one crashes, so they're hidden, never closed. In the editor, "panel" means a rail section like Background.
- Floating windows (`windows/floating.rs`): the record bar and the recording preview. They follow you to the display you click on. One module shows, hides and places them and runs their pointer loop; each window only says what to do when the pointer moves.
- Pointer hover (`usePointerHover`), also called the hover bridge: how hover works in windows that get no mouse moves (the preview and the overlays). Rust reports the pointer, and the page marks the element under it with `data-hovered`, which every `hover:` style matches too.
- System-wide keys (`KeySet`): keys the app takes for a while because its windows aren't focused. Each key set handles its own keys. The picker takes Esc, Return and Tab (`PickerKey`), and Return and Tab go to every overlay: the one holding the Area box acts, or else the one under the pointer. The preview takes E, ⌘C and ⌘S (`PreviewKey`) while the pointer is over it. The countdown takes Esc (`CountdownKey`).
- Own windows: this app's windows (the record bar, the overlays, the preview, the frame and alerts). Captures always leave them out. Display and area captures leave out the whole app, so windows opened mid-recording stay out too.
- Cursor log (`capture/cursor_log.rs`): the cursor position and mouse buttons, polled during a recording and written as JSON Lines next to the video. Polling needs no permission. The editor draws the cursor from it and suggests zooms and speed-ups.
- Camera file (`camera.mov`): when a camera is chosen (not possible while the camera button is grayed out), the camera recorded at 1280×720 with no sound beside the screen video. It starts and ends with the screen and skips the same pauses, and `meta.json` names it under `camera`. Save, Copy and export leave it out until the camera bubble draws it.
- Bindings (`src/lib/bindings.ts`): TypeScript types generated from the Rust types by ts-rs. Don't edit them; run `bun run bindings`.
- Page event (`PageEvent`, `lib.rs`): every event Rust sends the pages, with what each carries. It's in the bindings, so a page that expects the wrong payload fails to compile.
- Rust value (`useRustValue`): a value Rust owns, like the recording status. A page asks for it once, then follows its event, and the first answer never overwrites a newer event.
- App storage: `~/Library/Application Support/com.aidankmcalister.opentape/recordings/`, a hidden scratch folder holding only the open recording, named `YYYY-MM-DD HH.MM.SS`, with its `project.json`. A folder left there at launch means the app crashed: `recover` keeps the newest quietly as the previous recording and deletes the rest. Nothing opens on its own. Restore previous recording (the bar's gear menu and the menu bar menu) opens it in the preview, and a new recording replaces it. Recovery leaves dev copies (from `dev:editor`, named after their source) alone, and `dev:editor` skips it.
